Best dark web monitoring tools for small business (2026)

Updated August 2026 · independent comparison · primary sources linked

The best dark web monitoring tool for most small businesses in 2026 is Have I Been Pwned: its email search and breach notifications are free, and domain-wide monitoring starts at about $4.39/month for one domain, the only option on this list with fully public, self-serve pricing. Step up to Flare or SpyCloud when you need stealer-log and malware-exfil data, and to Dark Web ID if you buy through a managed IT provider. Below, each tool with what it actually does, who it fits, and its real price.

How we ranked these

Three things decide the order: price transparency (can a small buyer see the cost without a sales call?), coverage (breach dumps only, or also infostealer/stealer-log and session data?), and honesty of claims (detection-and-alerting vs. impossible “removal” promises). 1. Have I Been Pwned, best free + cheapest paid

What it is: Troy Hunt’s breach-aggregation service. Its database currently holds 17,765,749,944 breached accounts across 1,022 breached websites (counts shown live on the HIBP homepage). Anyone can search a single email for free and subscribe to notifications. In recent years HIBP has also ingested stealer logs, so it surfaces some infostealer-harvested credentials, not just classic breach dumps.

Price: Free to search and be notified. Domain monitoring moved to paid subscriptions, “Core 1” is $4.39/mo (1 domain), “Core 3” is $36.99/mo (5 domains), scaling up for MSPs. See the API/subscription page.

Best for: Any small business that wants real monitoring without a sales call. Limits: It reports what it has ingested; it is not a managed service with an analyst, and it does not remediate for you.

2. Flare, broad dark web + stealer logs, quote-based

What it is: A threat-exposure platform covering dark-web forums, marketplaces, Telegram channels and stealer logs. Aimed at SMB through enterprise. Price: No public list price; you book a demo. Best for: Teams that want stealer-log coverage and a dashboard and are willing to talk to sales. Verify current scope and price on Flare’s own site.

3. SpyCloud, deepest recaptured data, enterprise pricing

What it is: An enterprise account-takeover-prevention platform built on a very large corpus of recaptured breach and malware-exfiltrated data, including session/cookie and infected-device data. Price: Enterprise sales quote, no public pricing. Best for: Mid-market and enterprise security teams; usually more than a small shop needs or can justify. This is the “$$$$ enterprise” end of the market.

4. Dark Web ID (Kaseya), the MSP channel option

What it is: A monitoring product sold primarily through managed service providers rather than direct. Watches for compromised domain and personal credentials. Price: No public pricing; you get it via your IT provider’s stack, so cost depends on their bundle. Best for: Businesses that already outsource IT to an MSP and want monitoring folded into that relationship. See our Dark Web ID comparison.

5. 1Password Watchtower, free with a password manager you may already have

What it is: Not a standalone monitor, a feature inside the 1Password apps that flags vault items appearing in known breaches (it uses the same k-anonymity breach data model HIBP pioneered). Price: Included with 1Password; team plans start around $19.95/mo for the minimum seats (annual, per user). Best for: Teams already standardising on a password manager, you get breach flags without buying a separate tool.

What about “the SMB tier between free and enterprise”?

Every buyer notices the same gap: free HIBP on one end, “call us” enterprise (SpyCloud, Dark Web ID, Flare) on the other, and not much in between with a published SMB price. HIBP’s Core tiers ($4.39, $36.99/mo) are the honest answer for domain monitoring today; a password manager’s built-in breach flags cover the individual-login side. Anything promising more should show you a price before it shows you a demo.

Reserve Flare/SpyCloud/Dark Web ID for when you genuinely need stealer-log depth or a managed relationship, and make them show you a price.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best free dark web monitoring tool for small business?
Have I Been Pwned. It lets anyone search an email address and subscribe to breach notifications at no cost, and it now includes some stealer-log data. Domain-wide monitoring is a paid add-on starting around $4.39/month, but the core search-and-notify function is genuinely free.
How much does dark web monitoring cost for a small business?
It ranges from $0 (HIBP free search and notifications, plus breached-password blocking built into Google Workspace and Microsoft 365) to about $4.39-$36.99/month for HIBP domain monitoring. Enterprise tools such as SpyCloud, Flare and Dark Web ID do not publish prices and require a sales quote.
Which dark web monitoring services publish their prices?
As of 2026, Have I Been Pwned publishes full self-serve pricing and 1Password publishes plan pricing. Dark Web ID (Kaseya), SpyCloud and Flare do not list prices publicly and route buyers through a quote or an MSP.
Do I need a dedicated tool if I already use a password manager?
Often not, to start. Password managers like 1Password include Watchtower-style breach flags that reuse the same breach data. A dedicated monitor adds continuous domain-wide alerting and, at the higher end, stealer-log and session-data coverage.
