Dark web monitoring pricing compared: what each tier really costs (2026)

Updated August 2026 · independent comparison · primary sources linked

Here is what leaked-credential / dark web monitoring actually costs a small business in 2026. Two options publish real prices: Have I Been Pwned (free to search and notify; domain monitoring from $4.39/month) and 1Password (breach flags bundled with the password manager). The enterprise tools, Dark Web ID, SpyCloud, Flare, do not publish prices and require a sales quote or an MSP. Below, every tier we could verify, and an honest read on the “call us” options.

Why the enterprise tools hide their price

Dark Web ID, SpyCloud and Flare route buyers through a demo or an MSP because their pricing is scoped per deployment, number of assets, data feeds, integrations, seats. That’s normal for enterprise software, but it has a consequence for a small buyer: you can’t comparison-shop without booking calls, and the floor is usually well above SMB self-serve pricing. When you do get a quote, ask what it includes (stealer-log data? session/cookie coverage? personal + domain? managed triage?) so you can compare like-for-like against the ~$4.39, $36.99/mo you’d pay HIBP.

Frequently asked questions

How much does dark web monitoring cost for a small business in 2026?
From $0 to about $37/month for transparent options. Have I Been Pwned is free to search and notify, with domain monitoring at $4.39/month (Core 1) up to $36.99/month (Core 3, five domains). Password managers include breach flags in their normal plans. Dark Web ID, SpyCloud and Flare do not publish prices and require a quote.
Which dark web monitoring tools have public pricing?
Have I Been Pwned publishes full self-serve tiers, and 1Password publishes plan pricing that includes Watchtower breach flags. Dark Web ID (Kaseya), SpyCloud and Flare do not list prices; they route buyers through a sales quote or an MSP.
Is paying for dark web monitoring worth it versus free?
The jump from free to HIBP's ~$4.39/month tier buys continuous, automatic domain-wide alerting instead of manual checks - high value for a low cost. The much pricier enterprise tools are only worth it when you need stealer-log/session depth or a managed service, which most small businesses don't at first.
